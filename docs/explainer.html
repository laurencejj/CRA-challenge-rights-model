Model primer · challenge rights paper · draft 16 Oct 2026

What happens when the regulator can say "that rating is too high"?

US life insurers can set their capital using ratings they buy privately. Since 2026 the NAIC's credit team can challenge a rating that sits 3 or more notches above its own view. This page builds our model of that rule from zero. Move the sliders to see each piece work.

Step 1 · The cast

Three players and one missing referee

The insurer buys a private bond and pays an agency to rate it. The rating sets the insurer's capital. Everyone on the private side gains from a high rating, so only the regulator's check pushes the other way.

Step 3 · The building blocks

Four lines of algebra

Everything is measured in notches. The agency knows a bond's true quality θ and chooses how far to inflate it. The SVO sees the bond too, but with error.

The rating and the SVO's view

r = θ + k   ·   s = θ + ε,  ε ~ N(0, σ²)

k is inflation. σ is how noisy the SVO's own analysis is. The SVO measures the gap g = r − s = k − ε.

The tripwire

P(k) = q · Φ( (k − τ) / σ )

A challenge starts when the gap reaches τ = 3 and is upheld with probability q. Noise turns a hard line into a fuzzy one.

The agency's payoff

U(k) = b·k·(1 − P(k))  −  L·P(k)  −  ½·γ·k²
  • b·k·(1 − P): the fee gain from inflating, kept only if the rating survives.
  • L·P: the agency's loss if a challenge is upheld (lost client, scrutiny, risk to its NAIC recognition).
  • ½·γ·k²: everyday reputation cost. Large agencies have a higher γ than small ones.
  • Without the rule the agency picks k₀ = b/γ. We set this to match the observed pre-rule gaps.

Step 5 · Many deals

The bunching prediction

Now take 6,000 simulated deals. 86% are rated by small agencies, whose pre-rule gap averages about 3 notches, and 14% by large ones, about 1.9. These shares and gaps come from the NAIC's 2023 sample. The surprise in this model: a sharper SVO lets agencies sit closer to the line, so average inflation is higher when σ is small. A noisier SVO deters more but challenges more honest ratings. That trade-off drives the design question in Stage 3.
